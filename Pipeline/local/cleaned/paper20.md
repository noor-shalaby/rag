## Prevalence and Associated Factors of Laparoscopic Versus Open Appendectomy among Insured Patients in Yemen 20222024
Adel Ahmed Al-Emad 1 , Essam Hamdi Alsafadi 2 and Youssef Adel Al-Emad 1
1 Community and Public Health, Faculty of Medicine and Health Science, Sanaa University, Sanaa, Yemen.
2 Community and Family Medicine, Faculty of Medicine and Health Science, UST, Sanaa, Yemen.
## ABSTRACT
Background: Acute appendicitis is a common surgical emergency, with appendectomy being the standard treatment. The choice between laparoscopic appendectomy (LA) and open appendectomy (OA) remains a topic of debate, particularly in resource-limited settings like Yemen.
Aim: To compare the prevalence and descriptive patterns and crude associations of laparoscopic versus OA among insured patients in Yemen from 2022 to 2024.
Methods: A retrospective comparative study was conducted using data from 243 patients who underwent appendectomy (either LA or OA) at more than 40 hospitals in Yemen. Data was extracted from the medical records of patients with health insurance at Medical Insurance Specialist Company. The study compared demographic characteristics, hospital stay duration, cost, and factors influencing the choice of surgical approach.
Results: The prevalence of OA (60.5%) was significantly higher than that of LA (39.5%). Patients undergoing OA were more likely to present with complicated appendicitis (90.9% vs. 9.1% for LA, P <0.001). Residents of Sana'a City were more likely to undergo OA compared to those from other regions (COR = 8.306, 95% CI: 2.861-24.115, P <0.001). LA was associated with higher costs (median cost: 179900 YR) compared to OA (median cost: 135000 YR; P <0.001). However, there was no significant difference in hospital stay duration between LA and OA ( P =0.676). The majority of patients (81.9%) presented with uncomplicated appendicitis, and 50.6% were discharged within one day.
Conclusions: OA remains the predominant surgical approach in Yemen, particularly for complex cases and in urban areas such as Sana'a.

## INTRODUCTION
Acute appendicitis is one of the most common causes of acute abdomen. Acute appendicitis is relatively rare in infants, and becomes increasingly common in childhood and early adult life, reaching a peak incidence in the teens and early 20s. The incidence of appendicitis is equal among males and females before puberty [1,2] .
Appendicitis is an inflammation of the appendix, a major surgical cause of abdominal pain [3] . It can be classified into uncomplicated and complicated forms. Prompt diagnosis is crucial to reducing complications.
The etiology is multifactorial leads to obstruction of the appendiceal lumen. The most common causes of luminal obstruction include lymphoid hyperplasia secondary to inflammatory bowel disease or infections (more common during childhood and in young adults), fecal stasis and fecaliths (more common in the elderly), parasites, or, more rarely, foreign bodies and neoplasms [1] .
Diagnosis relies on history and physical examination. Classic symptoms include migrating pain from the periumbilical region to the right iliac fossa, worsening with movement or cough, accompanied by nausea, vomiting, constipation, and urinary symptoms [1] . Key diagnostic features include localized abdominal tenderness, muscle

The Alvarado score helps in diagnosis; a score of 7 or higher indicates a high probability of appendicitis. Ultrasound and computed tomography (CT) scans assist in ambiguous cases, with CT having a higher accuracy [4] .
Complicated appendicitis, including conditions such as perforation, gangrene, or abscess formation, often presents with unusual symptoms and necessitates prompt intervention to prevent severe complications. Accurate preoperative diagnosis is crucial as it influences the urgency and type of treatment, impacting the risk of complications and the postoperative recovery [5] .
Laparoscopic appendectomy (LA) has become a widely accepted method for diagnosing and treating acute appendicitis, offering advantages such as better wound healing, reduced postoperative pain, quicker return to diet, earlier hospital discharge, and improved cosmetic outcomes [6,7] .
The choice between LA and open appendectomy (OA) is influenced by the risks and benefits of each procedure, including infection rates, postoperative pain, and recovery time. The average incision size for OA is 3.5 cm, while for LA it is 3 cm. Pain levels, measured on a 1-10 scale, are similar between the two methods, with both groups reporting an average pain score of 6 [8,9] .
This study provides real-world evidence from a resource-limited healthcare setting, highlighting a gap between current surgical practice and international recommendations. Guidelines from the World Society of Emergency Surgery and the Society of American Gastrointestinal and Endoscopic Surgeons recommend laparoscopic appendectomy as the preferred approach for most uncomplicated and many complicated cases when feasible; however, open appendectomy remains predominant in Yemen, particularly in complicated presentations, likely due to infrastructure, insurance, and resource constraints rather than clinical factors alone. These findings underscore practice-guideline discrepancies and support the need for national guidelines, surgical audit systems, expanded laparoscopic training, and policy reforms to enhance equitable access to minimally invasive surgery in Yemen and similar low- and middle-income countries.
This study aimed to compare laparoscopic and OA in appendicitis in Yemen.
## PATIENTS AND METHODS
This retrospective comparative study was carried out on 243 patients who had health insurance at the Medical Insurance Specialist company and underwent appendectomy (either laparoscopic or open).
## ETHICAL APPROVAL
The study was conducted from January 2022 to May 2024, with approval from the Ethical Committee of Sana'a University, Yemen (Approval No: A 0122/1444).
Exclusion criteria were patients who had appendectomy combined with other major surgeries, incomplete medical records, and postoperative diagnosis other than acute appendicitis.
## STUDY AREA AND PERIOD
The study was conducted at the first and largest Medical Insurance Company in Yemen, headquartered in Sana`a and with branches in all Yemeni governorates, during the period from January 2022 to May 2024. This was addressed in a variety of patient populations, healthcare resources, and clinical practice settings.
## SAMPLING METHOD
We used a consecutive sampling method. This involved reviewing and analyzing existing medical reports of all patients who underwent appendectomy (either laparoscopic or open) within the specified period. This method ensured the inclusion of all eligible cases and provided a complete dataset for comparison.
## Surgical Approach Selection
The attending surgeon determined the surgical approach (laparoscopic or open appendectomy) as part of routine clinical practice and was not guided by a standardized study protocol. In accordance with published guidelines, the decision was based on preoperative clinical assessment, including patient history, physical examination, laboratory findings, imaging results, and classification of appendicitis as uncomplicated or complicated. Additional factors influencing the choice included surgeon experience and preference, availability of laparoscopic equipment, institutional resources, patient clinical stability, and insurance authorization policies. As this was a retrospective review, the specific rationale for selecting the surgical approach was not consistently documented and could not be independently evaluated.
## Data Collection Methods and Tools
The study retrospectively reviewed the medical reports of patients who underwent appendectomy during the period and within the study area. We used data extraction forms and spreadsheets to collect and organize the data systematically. We obtained access to the medical reports of patients insured through the Medical Insurance Specialist.

## DISCUSSION
The study revealed that OA was more prevalent than LA among the study population. Specifically, OA was performed in 60.5% of cases, while LA accounted for 39.5% of the procedures. NASCIMENTO et al. [10] revealed that 53,024 appendectomies were performed in the public health services in Bahia, of which 94.9% were open surgeries.
The higher prevalence of OA may reflect its widespread use in settings where laparoscopic equipment or specialized surgical expertise is limited. Conversely, the significant proportion of LA cases suggests a growing adoption of minimally invasive techniques, which are often associated with shorter recovery times and reduced postoperative complications [11] . However, the lower utilization of LA may also indicate barriers such as cost, infrastructure limitations, or lack of training among healthcare providers.
In the current study, reveals no significant association. The proportion of male participants in the OA group was 61.9% ( n =91/147), while in the LA group, it was 59.4% ( n =57/96). Conversely, female participants constituted 38.1% ( n =56/147) in the OA group and 40.6% ( n =39/96) in the LA group. The p-value of 0.693 indicates that the observed differences in sex distribution between the two surgical groups were not statistically significant ( P &gt; 0.05).
Given these findings, it can be inferred that sex does not appear to be a differentiating factor in the allocation of patients to either OA or LA procedures within this study cohort. This lack of association suggests that other clinical or demographic variables, rather than sex, may influence the choice of surgical approach. Further research is warranted to explore additional factors that could contribute to the selection of open versus LA. Similar study conducted in Egypt by Ahmad et al. [12] showed that both type of operations higher among male participation than female (62.96% in OA and 61.11% in LA).
In this study, specifically, in complicated cases, OA was performed in 90.9% of patients, while LA was utilized in only 9.1% of cases. This stark contrast is reflected in the COR of 8.598 (95% CI: 2.964-24.940), indicating that OA is significantly more likely to be performed in complicated presentations compared to LA. A similar study was conducted in the United States by Monrabal Lezama et al. [13] 82.7% of OA was performed for complicated appendicitis. Another study by Iqbal et al. [14] In Bangladesh, 23.3% of OA was performed in complicated appendicitis compared to 8.5% in LA. In addition, OA is a straightforward procedure that can be performed without specialized equipment, making it accessible in various settings [15] .
In contrast, Zamaray et al. [16] In a previous study in the Netherlands, the majority of complicated appendicitis cases were operated on laparoscopically, 78%. Another study, Alarfai et al. [17] in Saudi Arabia complicated or uncomplicated appendicitis didn`t differ statistically between open and LA, complicated appendicitis was found 7.9% in LA compared to 5.8% in OA.
Our study demonstrates no significant association. In the OA group, 51.7% ( n =76/147) of patients were discharged within one day, while 48.3% ( n =71/147) remained hospitalized for more than one day. Similarly, in the LA group, 49.0% ( n =47/96) had a hospital stay of ≤1 day, compared to 51.0% ( n =49/96) with a stay exceeding one day. The p-value of 0.676 indicates that the difference in hospitalization duration between the two surgical groups was not statistically significant.
These findings suggest that the choice of surgical technique, whether OA or LA, does not significantly influence the length of hospital stay in this patient cohort. Given that both groups exhibited comparable proportions of short and extended hospitalization, other factors, such as postoperative complications, patient comorbidities, or institutional protocols, may play a more substantial role in determining recovery time. Further investigation is necessary to identify the underlying determinants of hospital stay duration following appendectomy. These findings were similar to those of Fujishiro et al. [18] , who reported that there was no significant association of the length of hospital stay between patients who underwent laparoscopic or OA. In contrast, Khirallah et al. [19] concluded that LA should be the initial procedure of choice for most cases of uncomplicated appendicitis.
The data reveal a strong association between patients' residence and the type of operation they undergo. Patients residing in Sana'a City were significantly more likely to undergo OA than LA, with a COR of 8.306 (95% CI: 2.861-24.115, P &lt;0.001). The higher proportion of OA among patients from Sana'a is likely due to referral patterns and insurance rules rather than to geographical location. This indicates that the odds of undergoing OA were approximately 8.3 times higher among residents of Sanaa City than among those from other areas, who served as the reference group. In contrast, patients from areas other than Sanaa City demonstrated a markedly different pattern, with 90.7% undergoing OA and only 9.3% undergoing LA. This stark difference is likely due to the referral pattern and insurance rules rather than geographical location. The statistically significant P-value (&lt;0.001) further reinforces the robustness of this association, suggesting that the observed relationship is unlikely to be due to chance. Moreover, the 95% confidence interval for the COR (2.86124.115) does not include the null value of 1, which further supports the strength and reliability of the association. This wide range, however, also indicates variability in the magnitude of the effect, which may warrant further investigation into potential confounding factors or underlying mechanisms driving this disparity. In contrast, Golz et al. [20] revealed that socioeconomic advantages, such as higher income and secondary education, are strongly associated with lower incidence of both operations.
In our study, the results indicate a statistically significant difference in cost between the two types of operations. Specifically, the median cost for OA was 135000 Yemeni currency (YR), while the median cost for LA was 179900 YR. Our result was similar to Alejandro Ruiz-Patiño [21] obtained from Colombian which demonstrated this study LA is a cost-effective alternative over OA with an increasing cost of $25.85 per day of additional hospitalization due to complications saved. Also, NASCIMENTO et al. [10] found that there was no difference in the medians of costs nor hospital services, per procedure.
The statistically significant ( P &lt;0.001) underscores the robustness of this finding, indicating that the observed difference in cost is highly unlikely to be due to random variation.
Interpretation of the observed associations should be cautious due to potential confounding inherent in the retrospective design. Factors such as surgeon experience, hospital resources, equipment availability, insurance approval, and disease severity may have influenced both the choice of surgical approach and outcomes. The higher use of open appendectomy in complicated cases likely reflects clinical selection rather than procedural superiority. Likewise, the lack of difference in hospital stay between laparoscopic and open appendectomy, which contrasts with some published studies, may be explained by institutional discharge practices and resource limitations rather than surgical technique alone. The identified associations do not imply causation, as the allocation of surgical approaches was non-randomized.
This study has several limitations. Referral- and accessrelated factors, such as insurance approval processes, hospital infrastructure, availability of laparoscopic equipment, and surgeon expertise, were not directly measured; therefore, the higher frequency of OA in Sana'a is likely attributable to referral patterns and insurance rules rather than geographical location. Additionally, as the study was limited to insured patients and hospitals with laparoscopic capability, the findings cannot be generalized to uninsured populations, rural communities, or public hospitals without laparoscopic facilities.
The decision-making pathways influencing the choice of surgical approach were not explicitly captured in this study. Factors such as surgeon preference, hospital capability, patient choice, and insurance coverage policies may have confounded the observed associations between patient characteristics and the type of appendectomy. As these exposure-related variables were not measured, causal inferences regarding determinants of surgical approach should be interpreted with caution.
Future multicenter studies, including broader healthcare settings, are recommended to improve national representativeness.
## CONCLUSIONS
This study highlights key differences between LA and OA among insured patients in Yemen, mainly in Sana'a. OA was more common (60.5%) than LA (39.5%), particularly in complicated cases (90.9%), likely due to limited laparoscopic resources, expertise, and cost. The cohort was predominantly young (mean 28.7 yrs) and male (60.9%), with 82.3% from Sana'a, reflecting regional healthcare disparities. No significant difference in hospital stay was observed, while LA was costlier upfront but may reduce long-term complications. Surgical choice was strongly associated with residence; however, the higher prevalence of OA in Sana'a is likely due to referral patterns and insurance rules rather than geographical location.
