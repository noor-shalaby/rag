## The Surgical Versus Non-Surgical Management of The Appendicular Abscess: A Prospective Cohort Study
## Ahmed Saada a , Makarios Moheb Sedky Dawood a* , Mohamed A. Omar a , Ahmed Alsabaqy Abdulwareth b
- a Department of General Surgery, Faculty of Medicine, Qena University, Qena, Egypt.
- b Department of Diagnostic and Intervention Radiology,Faculty of Medicine , Qena University, Qena, Egypt.
## Abstract
Background : Appendicular abscess represents a complex manifestation of appendicitis, traditionally managed with emergency surgery or initial conservative treatment. Minimally invasive percutaneous drainage combined with antibiotics has emerged as an alternative, potentially reducing morbidity.
Objectives: This study aimed to compare clinical outcomes, postoperative recovery, and complications between surgical and percutaneous management of appendicular abscess.
Patients and methods : A prospective cohort study was conducted at Qena University Hospitals from August 2024 to August 2025, including 53 adults with radiologically confirmed appendicular abscess. Patients were allocated to surgical drainage (n = 31) or ultrasound-guided percutaneous drainage with antibiotics (n = 22). Allocation was by surgical team and based on patient selection and predefined clinical and radiological criteria. Preoperative assessment included clinical evaluation, laboratory tests, and imaging. Procedural details, drain management, hospital stay, time to oral intake, complications, and readmissions were recorded.
Results: The median patient age was 35(16-72) years, with 56.6% male. Percutaneous drainage was associated with significantly shorter operative time(100(55-160) vs. 15(7-45) min; p < 0.001), earlier drain removal(4(2-14) vs. 2(2-5) days; p=0.002), reduced hospital stay(5(3-17) vs. 3(2-7) days; p=0.002), and faster resumption of oral intake(4(4-6) vs. 24(24-48) h; p < 0.001). Postoperative complications, particularly wound-related events, were significantly lower in the percutaneous group(p < 0.001). Readmission rates were higher after percutaneous drainage(50%) for elective interval appendectomy.
Conclusion: Ultrasound-guided percutaneous drainage with antibiotics offers faster recovery and lower wound morbidity than surgical drainage for appendicular abscess, though some patients may still require interval appendectomy.
Keywords: Appendicular abscess; Percutaneous drainage; Surgical drainage.

## Introduction
Appendicitis is a leading cause of acute abdominal pain requiring surgical intervention (Brown et al., 2012; Das et al., 2019). In 2 -7% of cases, appendicitis presents with more complex manifestations, including abscess or inflammatory mass formation (Lind, 2019).
Emergency surgical intervention in these patients is often avoided due to increased risks, such as widespread intra-abdominal inflammation, intestinal adhesions, postoperative sepsis, and delayed wound healing (Thomson et al., 2015). Consequently, several authors have recommended initial conservative management, including ultrasound-guided percutaneous drainage combined with antibiotic therapy, followed by interval appendectomy when indicated (Habib et al., 2018) . Evidence suggests that the recurrence rate after successful conservative treatment is approximately 7%, indicating that interval appendectomy may not be routinely required (Darwazeh et al., 2016).
This study was conducted to compare clinical outcomes, postoperative morbidity, and hospital stay in patients managed with emergency surgery versus those treated with percutaneous drainage for appendicular abscess.
## Patients and methods Study Design
This was a prospective cohort study conducted to compare surgical and non-surgical management of appendicular abscesses. The study was performed at Qena University Hospitals, encompassing both the General Surgery and Radiology Departments. Patients were recruited from the hospital's emergency room between August 1, 2024, and August 1, 2025.
## Inclusion Criteria
Patients were eligible for inclusion if they were adults presenting with a clinically and radiologically confirmed appendicular abscess and able to provide informed consent. Suitability for either surgical drainage or ultrasound-guided percutaneous drainage was determined by the treating surgical team based on patient selection and predefined clinical and radiological criteria, including abscess size, location, complexity (e.g., multiloculated collections), presence of generalized peritonitis, patient comorbidities, and overall operative risk.
Patients were excluded if suspected of having underlying ileocecal disease or malignancy, had contraindications to anesthesia or antibiotic therapy, or if they declined to provide informed consent. Also, the immunocompromised patients and patients on cortisone or on immunosuppressive drugs were excluded from the study.
## Preoperative Assessment
All participants underwent a detailed preoperative assessment. Patient history included symptom onset, duration, and prior medical or surgical conditions. Comprehensive physical examination assessed vital signs, abdominal tenderness, and signs of localized or systemic infection. Laboratory investigations included complete blood count (CBC), random blood glucose, renal function tests (blood urea and creatinine), coagulation profile (prothrombin time and concentration), CRP, electrolytes, and screening for infectious diseases (HBsAg, anti-HCV, HIV antibody).
Diagnostic imaging included erect abdominal Xrays to rule out perforation or obstruction, ultrasonography to confirm abscess size and location, and CT scans in selected cases for detailed evaluation.
## Patient Allocation and Management
Patients were allocated into two groups based on treatment modality: Group A underwent surgical drainage, and Group B received percutaneous drainage with interventional radiology and antibiotics. Patients were allocated to either group with the process standardized in the study protocol to ensure consistency and minimize selection bias.
Surgical Drainage (Group A): Surgical procedures were performed under general anesthesia via a McBurney or right paramedian incision. The abscess was evacuated, the cavity lavaged, and an appendectomy performed when feasible. Drains (20 French) were inserted, anchored, and wounds closed. Daily drain output, wound inspection, and dressing changes using povidone-iodine were recorded until discharge.
Percutaneous Drainage (Group B): Under local anesthesia with lignocaine hydrochloride, the abscess was punctured using an 18-G needle
under ultrasound guidance. Pus was aspirated for culture and sensitivity, followed by insertion of a pigtail catheter (up to 12 French) using wireguided placement. The catheter was secured to the skin and connected to an evacuation bag. Daily saline washouts with metronidazole were performed, and drainage output was monitored

## Postoperative and Follow-Up
Postoperative care included monitoring the length of hospital stay, time to symptom resolution, and tolerance of oral feeding. Patients were assessed daily for fever, leukocytosis, and drain output. Ultrasound evaluations were performed every other day or as indicated. Criteria for operative drain or catheter removal included minimal output (<5 ml serous fluid for three consecutive days) and ultrasound confirmation of complete abscess evacuation.
## Ethical consideration
This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Faculty of Medicine, South Valley University, under ethical code ( SVU-MEDSUR011-1-24-9-937) . Written informed consent was obtained from all participants. The study conformed to the ethical standards of the Declaration of Helsinki and ensured confidentiality, voluntary participation, and the right to withdraw at any point.

## Statistical analysis
Data analysis was performed using SPSS version 26. Continuous variables are presented as median (range) and tested for normality using the Shapiro -Wilk test. Comparisons of continuous variables were conducted using the Mann -Whitney U test. Categorical variables are expressed as frequencies and percentages, with group comparisons made using the Chi-square or Fisher's exact test as appropriate. A p -value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

## Results
A total of 53 patients with appendicular abscess were included, with a median age of 35 (16 - 72) years; 30 (56.6%) were male and 23 (43.4%) were female. At admission, systolic (120 (90 - 150) mmHg) and diastolic (80 (60 - 100) mmHg) blood pressures were within the normal range. Respiratory rate was slightly elevated at 21 (17 26) cycles/min, whereas oxygen saturation remained normal (0.97 (0.94 - 0.99) %). The median temperature was mildly elevated at 38.1 (37.5 - 40) °C, with a corresponding increase in heart rate (96 (78 - 120) bpm). The median duration of pain before admission was 5 (3 - 20) days. Laboratory findings showed marked leucocytosis (12700 (4300 - 50000)/ μL), with neutrophilia (82 (67.3 - 96) %) and relative lymphopenia (14.6 (2.5 - 25) %), yielding an elevated neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (5.5 (3 38.4)). Haemoglobin (12.4 (10.7 - 14.1) g/dL), renal function tests (blood urea 30 (15 - 43) mg/dL; serum creatinine 0.9 (0.3 - 1.3) mg/dL), and electrolytes (sodium 135 (122 - 144) mEq/L) were within normal limits, although serum albumin was mildly reduced (3.5 (2 - 4.6) g/dL). Inflammatory markers were markedly elevated, including ESR at 119 (50 - 200) mm/hr (first hour) and 65 (30 - 90) mm/hr (second hour), C-reactive protein at 150 (48 - 384) mg/L, and CRP/albumin ratio at 42.8 (10.4 - 97.1) The mean abscess size was 4.5 ± 1.4 cm with range (2 -10). (Table 1). Appendectomy was performed in all patients managed surgically (31 [100%]) compared with 13 (59%) in the percutaneous group (p < .001) (Fig 3).
Operative time was substantially longer in the surgical group (100 (55 - 160) min) than in the percutaneous group (15 (7 - 45) min; p < .001). Drain type differed significantly by treatment modality: all surgical patients received an open 20 French drain, whereas all percutaneous cases were managed with a 12 French pigtail catheter (p < .001). Median daily drain output was higher in the percutaneous group (140 (10 - 250) mL/day) than in the surgical group (90 (60 - 200) mL/day; p < .001). Drains were removed earlier following percutaneous drainage (2 (2 - 5) days) compared
with surgical management (4 (2 - 14) days; p = 0.002) (Table 2) .
Clinical recovery favored percutaneous drainage. Hospital stay was shorter in the percutaneous group (3 (2 - 7) days) compared with the surgical group (5 (3 - 17) days; p = 0.002). Oral feeding was resumed markedly earlier after percutaneous management (4 (4 - 6) h) than after surgery (24 (24 - 48) h; p < .001). Fever resolved in most patients, with no significant difference between groups (100.0% vs 90.3%; p = 0.133), although duration of fever was longer after surgery (3 (2 3) vs 2 (1 - 2) days; p < .001). (Table 3) .
Postoperative complications were more frequent after surgery. Ileus occurred in 2 (6.5%) surgical patients, managed conservatively, but was absent in the percutaneous group (p = 0.505). Residual collections were significantly more common following surgery (13 [41.9%]) than percutaneous drainage (4 [18.2%]; p < 0.001). In the surgical group, 7 patients were managed conservatively with antibiotics, 4 patients required ultrasoundguided aspiration, and 2 patients underwent pigtail insertion. In the percutaneous group, 2 patients required reinsertion of a pigtail catheter, and 2
underwent conversion to open surgery after failed drainage. Wound complications occurred exclusively in the surgical group, including wound infection (7 [22.6%]) managed with dressings and antibiotics, and wound burst with infection (5 [16.1%]) requiring surgical debridement and closure (p < 0.001) (Table 4) . Readmission rates also differed significantly (p < 0.001). No readmission occurred in 25 (80.6%) surgical patients versus 11 (50.0%) in the percutaneous group. Elective appendectomy was the main cause of readmission after percutaneous drainage (11 [50.0%]), while wound-related surgical closure (5 [16.1%]) and conservative management with CT evaluation (1 [3.2%]) occurred only after surgery (Table 5) .
In patients managed conservatively, the possibility of misdiagnosing an underlying ileocecal pathology exists. Therefore, routine follow-up after resolution of the acute inflammatory process was mandatory, typically with computed tomography (CT) imaging, and in some cases, colonoscopy was warranted.

## Discussion
Management of appendicular abscess remains controversial. Traditionally, conservative treatment with antibiotics followed by interval appendectomy was recommended to avoid the risks of early surgery, such as fecal fistula (Khorshid et al. 2022) However, the necessity of interval appendectomy has been questioned, as conservative treatment can fail in 10 -20% of cases, requiring emergency surgery with higher morbidity, and recurrence may occur after discharge (Gignoux et al., 2018). Increasing evidence supports nonsurgical approaches such as antibiotics combined with image-guided percutaneous drainage, which have shown high success rates and low complication incidence (Sekine et al., 2021).
In this context, this study compared the outcomes of ultrasound-guided percutaneous drainage versus surgical drainage in patients presenting with appendicular abscess. Overall, percutaneous drainage was associated with shorter hospital stay, earlier return to oral feeding, faster resolution of fever, and lower readmission rates compared to surgical management. These findings support the growing evidence that minimally invasive approaches can provide effective source control with less morbidity in appropriately selected patients.
The demographic profile of our study population, with a median age of 35 (16 - 72) years and a male predominance (56.6%), aligns well with the established epidemiology of complicated appendicitis. This age distribution is consistent with previous reports, such as Mejri et al. (2021). The median duration of abdominal pain prior to presentation was 5 (3 - 20) days, reflecting the typical delayed presentation that favors abscess formation. On admission, patients demonstrated a moderate systemic inflammatory response, with elevated WBC count (12700 (4300 - 50000) / μL ) and fever (38.1 (37.5 -40) °C). Similarly, inflammatory markers were markedly elevated, with CRP averaging 150 (48 - 384) mg/L and ESR 65 (30 - 90) mm/hr. These findings are in line with previous descriptions of localized appendiceal perforation and abscess formation (Beltrán, 2015; Singh et al., 2024).
The mean abscess size was 4.5 ± 1.4 cm (range 2.0 -10.0 cm), representing an appropriate spectrum for both surgical and percutaneous drainage. Notably, this distribution corresponds with guideline recommendations that very small collections (&lt; 3 cm) may not be suitable for percutaneous drainage, while larger abscesses are generally amenable to both treatment approaches (Harclerode and Gnugnoli, 2025).
Our study demonstrated significant differences between percutaneous drainage and surgical management of appendicular abscess in both procedural and clinical outcomes. Percutaneous drainage was associated with significantly shorter procedure times (15 (7 - 45) vs. 100 (55 - 160) minutes, p &lt; 0.001), reflecting the minimally invasive nature of image-guided interventions. Similar results were observed by Negm et al. (2024) , who noted reduced operative durations in patients treated with conservative therapy.
Drain management also differed significantly between groups. Daily drain output was higher in the percutaneous cohort (140 (10 - 250) vs. 90 (60 - 200) mL/day, p &lt; 0.001), which may indicate more effective evacuation of abscess contents via the pigtail catheter or differences in abscess cavity characteristics. Despite higher output, drains were removed earlier in the percutaneous group (2 (2 5) vs. 4 (2 - 14) days, p = 0.002), suggesting more rapid resolution of the infectious process. While some prior studies have suggested longer drainage periods for percutaneous approaches (Chaiyasoot and Jaruthien, 2021). Our findings likely reflect the specific image-guided technique and tailored antibiotic regimen employed.
Percutaneous drainage also conferred clear clinical advantages. Hospital stay was significantly shorter in the percutaneous group (3 (2 - 7) vs. 5 (3 - 17) days, p = 0.002), reflecting faster recovery and reduced postoperative burden. Oral feeding was resumed markedly earlier (4(4 6) vs. 24 (24 - 48) hours, p &lt; 0.001), consistent with minimal bowel manipulation and reduced systemic inflammatory response. Fever resolved in all percutaneous cases compared with 90.3% of surgical cases, with a shorter duration (3 (2 - 3) vs 2 (1 - 2) days, p &lt; 0.001), indicating more effective source control. These findings are supported by prior studies reporting rapid clinical improvement with percutaneous drainage when combined with appropriate antibiotic therapy (Akingboye et al., 2021)
Our results regarding postoperative recovery are largely consistent with existing literature. Seif et al. (2015) , Zerem et al. (2007) , and Brown et al. (2003) similarly reported shorter hospitalization and faster recovery with percutaneous drainage and interval appendectomy. Habib et al. (2018) emphasized reduced morbidity and quicker functional recovery with conservative management compared to emergency appendectomy, highlighting how patient selection, timing, and institutional protocols may influence outcomes.
Overall, our findings emphasize the technical simplicity, reduced procedural burden, and faster clinical recovery associated with percutaneous drainage compared with immediate surgical management, while also illustrating that operative strategy must be tailored to patient characteristics and abscess complexity.
The complication profile in our study strongly favored the percutaneous approach. Overall, 81.8% of patients in the percutaneous group experienced no complications compared with only 51.6% in the surgical group (p &lt; 0.001), primarily due to the absence of wound-related complications. The surgical cohort experienced a 38.7% wound complication rate, including superficial infections and more serious wound dehiscence requiring surgical intervention. Ileus occurred exclusively in the surgical group (2 cases), reflecting the greater bowel manipulation
and peritoneal inflammation associated with open surgery.
Residual collections on ultrasound before discharge were observed in both groups, though their management differed. In the percutaneous group, residual collections were addressed with catheter reinsertion (2 cases) or conversion to surgery (2 cases). In the surgical group, 7 patients were managed conservatively with antibiotics, 4 patients required ultrasound-guided aspiration, and 2 patients underwent pigtail insertion. Our results are in agreement with Demetrashvili et al. (2019), who reported significantly fewer postoperative complications in patients managed conservatively with interval surgery compared to those undergoing emergency surgery (P = 0.02). Similarly, Habib et al. (2018) demonstrated lower complication rates, including wound infection and dehiscence, in patients treated with ultrasound-guided drainage compared to emergency appendectomy. Olsen et al. (2014) further support these findings, showing that complications such as wound infection, fecal fistula, and burst abdomen were significantly more frequent in surgically managed patients, whereas the percutaneous group experienced no major complications. Together, these data strongly support the safety advantage of percutaneous drainage over surgical drainage in appendiceal abscess.
An important observation in our cohort was the higher readmission rate in the percutaneous group (50.0% vs. 19.4%, p = 0.019), predominantly for elective interval appendectomy. This reflects current standard practice, where percutaneous drainage serves as a bridge to delayed appendectomy, allowing resolution of acute inflammation. Notably, 40.9% of percutaneous patients had atrophied appendices that did not require surgery, indicating that conservative management can be definitive in a substantial proportion of cases. These results align with previous studies, show high technical and clinical success rates for percutaneous drainage (Brown et al., 2012; Habib et al., 2018) , and support selective, rather than routine, interval appendectomy.
Limitations &amp; Recommendations: This study has some limitations that should be acknowledged. First, it was conducted at a single center with a relatively small sample size, which may limit the generalizability of our findings. Second, the nonrandomized design may introduce the possibility of selection bias in treatment allocation. Additionally, follow-up duration was limited, and thus long-term outcomes such as recurrence, late complications, and quality of life could not be fully assessed.
Future research should aim to address these gaps. Larger, multicenter randomized controlled trials are needed to confirm the superiority of percutaneous drainage over immediate surgical drainage in selected patients with appendicular abscess. A longer follow-up is also warranted to better evaluate recurrence rates and long-term morbidity. Moreover, standardized criteria for patient selection between percutaneous and surgical management should be developed to optimize outcomes and reduce variability in practice.
Conclusion: Our findings suggest that ultrasound-guided percutaneous drainage combined with antibiotics may offer advantages over surgical drainage for appendicular abscesses, including shorter hospital stay, earlier resumption of oral intake, and lower wound-related complications. While percutaneous management appears effective in achieving abscess resolution, some patients still require elective interval appendectomy.
